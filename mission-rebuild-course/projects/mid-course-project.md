# THE CIVILIAN REBUILD BLUEPRINT
## Mid-Course Project — Assigned After Module 5

---

## PROJECT OVERVIEW

You are at the halfway point. You've covered identity, structure, finances, career, and resilience. Now it's time to stop absorbing and start building.

This is not a quiz. This is not busywork. This is your actual life plan — the document you should be able to hand to someone and say, "This is who I am, where I'm going, and how I'm getting there."

The Civilian Rebuild Blueprint combines everything from Modules 1 through 5 into one complete, working document. By the time you finish it, you'll have a clearer picture of your civilian life than most people get after years of stumbling around. That clarity is earned — not given.

Do this right. Future you is counting on it.

---

## ESTIMATED COMPLETION TIME

**6 to 10 hours** across multiple sessions. Do not try to crank this out in one sitting. Some sections require honest reflection that takes time. Block it into your schedule like a mission brief — specific times, no distractions.

Suggested breakdown:
- Session 1 (90 min): Part 1 — Identity Map
- Session 2 (90 min): Part 2 — Daily Structure
- Session 3 (90 min): Part 3 — Financial Snapshot
- Session 4 (90 min): Part 4 — Career Transition Plan
- Session 5 (90 min): Part 5 — Resilience System
- Session 6 (60 min): Reflection + review + polish

## INSTRUCTIONS

Complete all five parts in order. Each part builds on the last. Write in complete sentences unless instructed otherwise. Be specific — vague answers like "get a good job" or "do better financially" will not pass. Honest, specific, and actionable is the standard.

You may type your responses directly into this document template or into a clean Word/Google Doc. Format it clearly. Label every section.

## PART 1: IDENTITY MAP

*Module Reference: Module 1 — Who You Are Now*

Your identity is the foundation. If this part is shaky, everything built on top of it will wobble. Take your time here.

---

### 1A. Who You Were Before the Military

Answer the following in 1-3 paragraphs:

- How old were you when you enlisted or commissioned? What was your life like at that point?
- What did you want? What were you running toward — or away from?
- What kind of person were you? What did you care about?
- What skills, habits, or traits did you bring with you into service?

> **Prompt:** Don't sanitize this. If you joined to get out of a bad situation, say so. If you had no plan and the recruiter caught you at the right moment, say so. Honesty here sets the tone for everything else.

---

### 1B. Who You Became in the Military

Answer the following in 1-3 paragraphs:

- What did the military make you better at?
- How did your identity change? What values, behaviors, or beliefs did you adopt?
- What roles did you hold? What were you known for among your peers?
- What are you most proud of from your service?
- What habits, mindsets, or patterns did the military give you that now cause friction in civilian life?

> **Prompt:** Both sides of this matter. The military built real strengths into you — and also may have installed some defaults that don't serve you anymore. Name both.

---

### 1C. Who You Are Choosing to Be Now

Answer the following in 1-3 paragraphs:

- Given everything above, who are you choosing to be as a civilian?
- What parts of your military identity do you keep? What do you leave behind?
- What new characteristics are you consciously building?
- How do you want people to describe you in five years?

> This is not about erasing your service. It's about deciding what goes with you and what stays at the gate.

---

### 1D. Core Values (Minimum 5)

List at least five core values. For each one:

- **State the value** (one to three words)
- **Define it in your own words** (2-4 sentences — what it means to *you*, not the dictionary definition)
- **Give one specific example** of how you live this value or intend to

**Format:**

```
VALUE: [Name]
DEFINITION: [Your personal definition]
EXAMPLE: [Specific behavioral example]
```

> **Important:** Do not list values you think sound good. List the ones that actually drive your decisions — even if they're uncomfortable to admit. Authenticity > optics.

---

### 1E. Civilian Identity Statement

Write a single, powerful paragraph (5-8 sentences) that answers: *Who are you right now, as a civilian?*

This statement should:
- Be written in first person
- Reference your service without being defined entirely by it
- Name your values
- Describe what you're building
- Sound like *you* — not a LinkedIn bio, not a performance review

> You will refine this statement in the Final Project. For now, make it real.

## PART 2: DAILY STRUCTURE

*Module Reference: Module 2 — Building Your Operating System*

Discipline creates freedom. But generic discipline doesn't work. Your operating system has to be built for you — your responsibilities, your energy patterns, your goals, your life.

---

### 2A. Your Personal Operating System

Map out your ideal daily structure across three blocks:

**Morning Routine**
- What time do you wake up?
- What are the first three things you do after waking?
- How long is your morning routine (start to work-ready)?
- What does it include? (Physical training, journaling, reading, cold exposure, prayer, meal prep — whatever applies to you)
- What is the purpose of your morning routine — what does it do for your mental and physical state?

**Work Blocks**
- When are your peak focus hours?
- How many focused work blocks do you have in a day?
- How long is each block?
- How do you protect them from interruption?
- What happens between blocks? (Rest, movement, food, admin tasks)

**Evening Routine**
- What time do you start winding down?
- What does your evening include?
- What time do you aim to be in bed?
- What is the purpose of your evening routine?

> If your current reality doesn't match your ideal, write both. Note the gap and what's causing it.

---

### 2B. Weekly Schedule Template

Build a realistic weekly schedule. Use the format below or recreate it in a table. Every major block of time should be accounted for.

```
MONDAY
0500-0600: [Activity]
0600-0800: [Activity]
0800-1200: [Activity]
1200-1300: [Activity]
1300-1700: [Activity]
1700-1900: [Activity]
1900-2100: [Activity]
2100+:     [Wind-down / Sleep]

[Repeat for Tuesday through Sunday]
```

Include:
- Work/job search/school hours
- Physical training
- Family/relationship time
- Learning blocks (course, reading, certifications)
- Admin time (finances, VA appointments, household)
- Recovery/downtime
- Flex time

> Your schedule doesn't have to be perfect. It has to be honest and functional. Build from where you actually are.

---

### 2C. Non-Negotiables

List 3 to 7 non-negotiable habits — things that happen every day regardless of mood, weather, motivation, or how bad yesterday was.

For each one:
- **Name the habit**
- **Frequency** (daily, 5x/week, etc.)
- **Why it's non-negotiable** (what does missing it cost you?)
- **Current status**: Locked in / Building / Inconsistent

**Format:**
```
HABIT: [Name]
FREQUENCY: [How often]
WHY: [What missing it costs you]
STATUS: [Locked In / Building / Inconsistent]
```

---

### 2D. Accountability System

Answer the following:

- Who or what holds you accountable? (Person, app, system, group — be specific)
- How do you track your habits and commitments? (Journal, app, spreadsheet, whiteboard)
- What happens when you miss a non-negotiable? What's your recovery protocol?
- What's the single biggest threat to your structure right now? How are you countering it?
