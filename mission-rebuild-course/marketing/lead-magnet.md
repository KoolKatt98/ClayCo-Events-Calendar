# SECTION 2: THE VETERAN REBUILD CHECKLIST

*Print this. Use a pen. Don't think too long on any item — your first instinct is usually the right one.*

**Marking system:**
- **[X]** = Done. Solid. This area is handled.
- **[~]** = Partially done or in progress. There's more work here.
- **[ ]** = Not done. This is a gap. Priority action required.

---

## AREA 1: IDENTITY CHECKLIST

*Who are you without the uniform? This is the question most veterans avoid — and the one that underlies every other challenge in transition.*

**1.** [ ] I can describe who I am without using my rank, branch, MOS, or unit as the primary descriptor.

**2.** [ ] I have written down (not just thought about) my top 5 personal values — the principles I actually live by, not the ones I think sound good.

**3.** [ ] I know the difference between my military identity (role, function, position) and my core identity (who I am as a human being). I can explain that difference out loud.

**4.** [ ] I have identified at least 3 skills or strengths that exist independent of the military — things I would have been good at regardless of whether I served.

**5.** [ ] When I introduce myself in civilian settings, I feel like my introduction accurately represents who I am — not like I'm hiding something or overselling something.

**6.** [ ] I have allowed myself to grieve the loss of my military identity — intentionally, not just by suppressing it. I know what I'm mourning and I've named it.

**7.** [ ] I have a written "operator's manual" or personal statement — something that describes my values, my strengths, the way I work best, and what I'm building toward.

**8.** [ ] I am not primarily defining myself by what I used to do. I have a forward-looking identity that is mine, not borrowed from my service record.

**9.** [ ] I have at least one community or context outside the military and veterans' world where I feel genuinely myself and genuinely valued.

**10.** [ ] I have asked — and begun to honestly answer — the question: "What do I want my life to mean in 10 years?" Not what do I want to achieve. What do I want it to mean.

---

## AREA 2: DISCIPLINE AND STRUCTURE CHECKLIST

*The military gave you structure from the outside. Civilian life requires you to build it from the inside. How's that going?*

**1.** [ ] I have a consistent morning routine — not a perfect one, not a 5am miracle routine — a real, repeatable sequence that starts my day with intention instead of chaos.

**2.** [ ] I have a weekly planning practice. Once a week, I sit down and look at the upcoming week — commitments, priorities, gaps. I'm not winging it day to day.

**3.** [ ] I know my top 3 priorities for this week. Right now. Without having to think about it for more than 10 seconds.

**4.** [ ] I use a system — calendar, task manager, notebook, something — to track what I need to do. My obligations are not all living in my head.

**5.** [ ] I have a consistent sleep schedule. I go to bed and wake up at roughly the same time most days. I'm not sleeping until noon on weekends and lying awake at 0300 on weekdays.

**6.** [ ] I exercise consistently — not perfectly, not at peak military fitness levels, but consistently. At least 3 times per week, most weeks.

**7.** [ ] I have a process for reviewing how the week went — what worked, what didn't, what I need to adjust. I'm iterating on my own systems.

**8.** [ ] I am not primarily reactive. I am not spending most of my time responding to whatever comes at me. I have protected time for the things that matter most.

**9.** [ ] I have identified my peak performance hours — the time of day when I do my best thinking and hardest work — and I protect those hours.

**10.** [ ] My environment supports my discipline. My phone, my physical space, my daily commitments, and my social environment are more likely to push me toward my goals than away from them.

---

## AREA 3: FINANCIAL BASELINE CHECKLIST

*You cannot build on a foundation you haven't measured. This section is about knowing your numbers — all of them.*

**1.** [ ] I know my exact monthly take-home income — all sources combined (employment, VA compensation, GI Bill housing allowance, rental income, spouse income, etc.).

**2.** [ ] I know my exact monthly fixed expenses — rent/mortgage, car payment, insurance, subscriptions, loan minimums. I could write the number right now without having to log into anything.

**3.** [ ] I track my variable spending. I know roughly what I spend on food, entertainment, clothing, and miscellaneous each month. This is not a guess — it's a number I can verify.

**4.** [ ] I have a written budget. Not a mental budget. A written-down, category-level plan for where my money goes each month. It may not be perfect, but it exists.

**5.** [ ] I have an emergency fund. At minimum, 1 month of expenses in a separate savings account that I do not touch. (Goal is 3–6 months, but 1 month is the floor.)

**6.** [ ] I know my net worth. Assets minus liabilities. I have calculated this number within the last 6 months. I know whether it went up or down.

**7.** [ ] I have no credit card debt that is older than 6 months. If I have credit card debt, I have a written plan to eliminate it with a specific payoff timeline.

**8.** [ ] I have contributed to a retirement or investment account in the last 12 months — even a small amount. The Thrift Savings Plan, a Roth IRA, or an employer 401k.

**9.** [ ] I have reviewed my credit report within the last 12 months. I know my score and I know what's on my report. (AnnualCreditReport.com is free.)

**10.** [ ] I have a 90-day financial goal that I am actively working toward. Not a vague wish — a specific, written goal with a dollar amount and a deadline.

---

## AREA 4: CAREER TRANSITION CHECKLIST

*Your military career is an asset — but only if you know how to deploy it in civilian markets. This section assesses your career readiness.*

**1.** [ ] I have a civilian resume — not a military resume converted to Times New Roman, but a document written in civilian language that accurately represents my experience and gets responses from civilian employers.

**2.** [ ] My LinkedIn profile is complete: professional photo, clear headline (not just my job title), a summary written in first person that tells my story, detailed work history, and at least 5 skills listed.

**3.** [ ] I have identified my target role or roles — the specific job titles I'm going for, the industries I'm targeting, and the companies or organizations on my list. I am not applying to everything.

**4.** [ ] I have researched the salary range for my target role in my target geography. I know what I'm worth in this market and I can name a number when asked.

**5.** [ ] I have had at least 3 informational conversations in the last 90 days with people who are in roles or industries I'm targeting. Not job interviews — conversations.

**6.** [ ] I can answer "Tell me about yourself" in 60–90 seconds in a way that is compelling, relevant, and does not begin with "I was in the Army for [X] years."

**7.** [ ] I can translate my most significant military accomplishment into civilian language — without acronyms, without rank structure, without assumed context — in under 2 minutes.

**8.** [ ] I have at least 3 people who can provide a professional reference and who are prepared to do so. I have confirmed their willingness in the last 6 months.

**9.** [ ] If I am in school on the GI Bill, I have a plan for what I am building toward with this degree — specific roles, specific outcomes, not just "I'm getting a degree."

**10.** [ ] I know what I will do next if my current career path doesn't work out. I have a Plan B that is realistic and that I have thought through, not just named.

## AREA 5: MENTAL RESILIENCE CHECKLIST

*This is not a mental health diagnostic. This is a practical assessment of the habits and systems that support your ability to function, adapt, and keep moving forward.*

**1.** [ ] I am sleeping 7–8 hours most nights. If I'm not, I have identified why and I'm actively working on it — not just accepting it as normal.

**2.** [ ] I move my body with some form of intentional exercise at least 3 times per week. Not because of appearance — because I know what happens to my mood and cognition when I don't move.

**3.** [ ] I have at least one person I can be completely honest with about how I'm actually doing — not a social answer, not "I'm fine." Someone who gets the real version.

**4.** [ ] I have an outlet for stress that is not alcohol, isolation, or substances. Physical exertion, creative practice, journaling, talking — something that actually processes stress rather than suppresses it.

**5.** [ ] I am honest with myself about my relationship with alcohol. I know how much I drink, I know whether it has increased since separation, and I am not lying to myself about the pattern.

**6.** [ ] If I am experiencing symptoms of PTSD, anxiety, depression, or TBI — I am getting support. Not suffering in silence. Not self-medicating. Getting actual support.

**7.** [ ] I have a recovery practice — something that recharges me after hard or draining days. I know what it is, and I use it deliberately rather than just collapsing.

**8.** [ ] I am engaged with something that gives me a sense of meaning or contribution. Not just a job — something I care about. It doesn't have to be big.

**9.** [ ] I have a relationship with my own anger. I know what triggers me, I know how I tend to respond, and I have strategies for managing high-activation states that don't damage the people around me.

**10.** [ ] I check in with myself deliberately — not just in crisis. I have some form of regular self-assessment practice (journaling, weekly review, therapy) that lets me catch problems before they become crises.

---

## AREA 6: VA BENEFITS CHECKLIST

*You earned every one of these. The question is whether you're claiming them.*

**1.** [ ] I am enrolled in VA healthcare. I have a primary care provider through the VA system, and I know how to access care when I need it.

**2.** [ ] I have filed a disability compensation claim. If I have service-connected conditions — injuries, illnesses, mental health conditions — I have filed, or I have a specific reason I have chosen not to.

**3.** [ ] I know my current disability rating. If I have one, I know the percentage and the monthly compensation amount. If I don't, I know why.

**4.** [ ] I have reviewed my rating in the last 2 years. If my conditions have worsened, or if I have conditions that were not included in the original claim, I have filed an increase or a new claim.

**5.** [ ] I have looked into Vocational Rehabilitation and Employment (VR&E / Chapter 31) as an alternative or supplement to the GI Bill — especially if I have a service-connected disability. I know what it covers and whether I'm eligible.

**6.** [ ] If I am using the GI Bill (Chapter 33 or Chapter 30), I know my remaining entitlement — to the month and day. I have a plan for how to use it before it expires.

**7.** [ ] I have investigated the VA home loan guarantee benefit. I know what it is, whether I'm eligible, how many times I can use it, and what the process looks like.

**8.** [ ] I have VA life insurance through VGLI or SGLI conversion. I know my coverage amount and my monthly premium. I have not let this lapse.

**9.** [ ] I know about Chapter 35 (Dependents' Educational Assistance) — if I am 100% P&T disabled, my dependents may be eligible for education benefits. If this applies to me, I have investigated it.

**10.** [ ] I have a Veterans Service Officer (VSO) or VA-accredited claims agent I can contact for help with claims — or I know how to find one for free. I am not navigating the VA system entirely alone.

## AREA 7: RELATIONSHIPS CHECKLIST

*The military gave you a built-in tribe. Civilian life did not. This section assesses the health of your relationships and the support structure around you.*

**1.** [ ] I have told the people closest to me — my spouse, partner, family — something real about how transition has actually been for me. Not the sanitized version. Something honest.

**2.** [ ] If I have a spouse or partner, we have had at least one serious conversation about what the transition has been like for both of us — what's changed, what's been hard, what we both need.

**3.** [ ] I have at least one close friendship outside the military — a person I am genuinely close to who did not serve, who knows me well, and who I can call on.

**4.** [ ] I have at least one veteran friend who I talk to honestly. Not a drinking buddy. Someone who is building their life and who is part of my accountability structure.

**5.** [ ] I have repaired or made an attempt to repair a significant relationship that took damage during my service — from deployment, from distance, from the way service changes a person.

**6.** [ ] If I have children, I am actively present — not just physically in the house, but engaged. I have specific time with my children that is uninterrupted and intentional.

**7.** [ ] I have identified toxic or draining relationships in my life — people who are consuming energy without contributing to my rebuild — and I have made conscious decisions about those relationships.

**8.** [ ] I have asked for help when I needed it in the last 6 months. Not struggled silently. Actually asked.

**9.** [ ] I know the difference between being self-sufficient and being isolated. I am not confusing strength with not needing anyone. I have people and I use them.

**10.** [ ] I have contributed to someone else's success or wellbeing in the last 30 days — not a grand gesture, just an act of genuine investment in another person's life. I am not only in receive mode.

---

## AREA 8: OWNERSHIP MINDSET CHECKLIST

*Are you building a life, or waiting for one to happen? This section is about radical ownership of your post-service reality.*

**1.** [ ] I have written down — on paper, not just in my head — what I want my life to look like in 1 year, 3 years, and 5 years. I have specifics, not just categories.

**2.** [ ] When something in my life is not working, my first response is to examine what I can change — not to explain why the situation is someone else's fault or beyond my control.

**3.** [ ] I have a 90-day written plan with specific, measurable goals and weekly action steps. Not a resolution. A plan.

**4.** [ ] I am taking one new risk or pursuing one new initiative right now — something that could fail, something with stakes, something I care about. I am not playing it safe because safe has stopped feeling like enough.

**5.** [ ] I have identified the one thing I keep procrastinating on — the phone call I haven't made, the claim I haven't filed, the conversation I've been avoiding — and I have put it on my calendar with a date.

**6.** [ ] I am not waiting to feel motivated before I take action. I act on schedule. I use the discipline I have. I know that motivation follows action, not the other way around.

**7.** [ ] I invest in my own development. Books, courses, coaching, mentorship, community — something. I am not relying on employers or institutions to develop me. I am developing myself.

**8.** [ ] I hold myself accountable with metrics. Not just effort, not just intentions — outcomes. I measure something. I know whether I am moving forward or standing still.

**9.** [ ] I know the difference between a circumstance and a choice. I can identify clearly which parts of my current situation are things that happened to me and which parts are choices I have made — even the hard ones to acknowledge.

**10.** [ ] I am building something. Not surviving. Not waiting. Building. I can name what it is, describe the next step, and tell you why it matters to me.

## HOW TO READ YOUR RESULTS

**Count your [X] marks across all 8 areas.**

- **64–80 [X]:** You are in active rebuild mode. You have solid foundations across most areas. The gaps you have are specific and fixable. Use the action items attached to your [ ] items and execute.
- **40–63 [X]:** You have real strengths and real gaps. You are functioning but you know you're leaving capability on the table. You need a system to address the [ ] items in a structured way — not all at once, but in a sequence.
- **20–39 [X]:** You are in early transition. Multiple foundational areas need work. This is not a crisis — this is an honest starting point. The most important thing you can do right now is get into a structured program that addresses all of these areas systematically, with support.
- **Under 20 [X]:** You are still in the drift phase. The good news is that you completed this checklist, which means you are ready to be honest — and that is the prerequisite for everything. Pick the area with the most items checked and start there. Then get help.

**The areas with the most [ ] marks are your priority order.**

This is not a score to feel good or bad about. It is intelligence. Use it like intel.
