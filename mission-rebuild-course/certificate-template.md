## SECTION 1: COMPLETION CRITERIA

### What a Student Must Complete to Earn the Certificate

The MISSION REBUILD™ Certificate of Completion is awarded to students who have demonstrated full engagement with the course — not just attendance, but active participation and produced work. The certificate represents a meaningful accomplishment. Treat it accordingly.

**Required for certification:**

**1. All 12 Modules Completed**
Students must complete all twelve modules of the course (Modules 0–11). Completion is defined as:
- Watching or attending all module content (video or live session)
- Submitting the module's reflection or discussion response
- Completing any self-assessment or exercise embedded in the module

A module is not considered complete based on attendance alone. Content engagement and output submission are both required.

**2. All Weekly Assignments Submitted**
Every week has a corresponding assignment. All assignments must be submitted to qualify for the certificate. Late submissions are accepted (see policy below) but must be received before the course close date, which is defined as 14 days after the final cohort session.

Assignments are graded on a 100-point scale. The minimum passing score per assignment is 70/100. Assignments scoring below 70 may be revised and resubmitted once, within 7 days of feedback.

**3. Mid-Course Project Submitted and Graded**
The mid-course project (assigned at Week 4, due at Week 6) is a major integration deliverable combining work from Modules 1–4. It must be:
- Submitted on time (or with an approved extension — see extension policy)
- Graded by the instructor or course facilitator
- Scored at or above the minimum passing threshold of 70/100

The mid-course project may be revised once if it scores below 70, provided the revised version is submitted within 10 days of feedback.

**4. Final Project Submitted and Graded**
The final project (assigned at Week 10, due at Week 12) is the capstone deliverable. It must be:
- Submitted on time (or with an approved extension)
- Graded by the instructor or course facilitator
- Scored at or above the minimum passing threshold of 70/100

The final project may be revised once if it scores below 70, provided the revised version is submitted within 14 days of feedback.

**5. Minimum Overall Passing Score: 75/100**
The overall course grade is calculated as follows:
- Weekly Assignments: 40% of total grade
- Mid-Course Project: 25% of total grade
- Final Project: 35% of total grade

A student must achieve a cumulative score of 75/100 or above across all components to earn the standard certificate. Students who meet all submission requirements but fall below 75 overall will be contacted by the instructor to discuss options (see appeals process below).

**6. Participation Requirements**
For live cohort deliveries, students must attend or watch recordings of a minimum of 9 out of 12 cohort calls. Watching a recording counts toward this requirement if the student submits a brief (3–5 sentence) response noting what they took from the session within 72 hours of the recording becoming available.

For fully self-paced students, participation is demonstrated through assignment submission, discussion board engagement, and completion of the peer feedback exercises included in the curriculum.

---

### What to Do If a Student Misses a Module or Assignment

**Missed Assignments (individual weeks):**
Students may submit up to two late assignments without penalty, provided they are submitted before the course close date (14 days after the final session). A third late assignment incurs a 10-point penalty on that assignment's grade. More than three missing assignments at course close date requires an extension agreement (see below) or results in an incomplete, with the student ineligible for the certificate in that cohort.

**Missed Module (partial engagement):**
If a student misses a full module — including the session and the assignment — they must make up all components to qualify for the certificate. The student should contact the instructor within 48 hours of the missed module to establish a make-up plan. Modules may be made up using recorded session content. Make-up submissions are due within 10 days of the original deadline.

**Extended Absence (two or more consecutive weeks):**
If a student must be absent for two or more consecutive weeks due to circumstances outside their control (medical emergency, family crisis, deployment, etc.), they should notify the instructor as soon as possible. Options include:
- A 2-week extension on all deliverables, allowing the student to complete the current cohort
- Deferral to the next available cohort with credit for completed work

Instructors should handle these situations with judgment and flexibility. The goal is not to enforce rules — the goal is to ensure students get the full course experience. Rubber-stamping extensions that enable chronic avoidance does not serve the student.

---

### Appeals Process for Borderline Cases

A borderline case is defined as a student who has met all submission requirements but whose final score falls within 5 points of the passing threshold (i.e., a score between 70–74 when the threshold is 75).

**Appeals process:**
1. The student submits a written appeal within 14 days of receiving their final grade. The appeal should include a brief explanation of any extenuating circumstances and a statement of what they feel they demonstrated through the course.
2. The instructor reviews the full body of work — not just the numeric score — including participation, the quality and depth of engagement across assignments, and any growth demonstrated over the course.
3. The instructor may:
   - Award the certificate on the basis of demonstrated learning and engagement
   - Offer the student one additional revision opportunity on the final project
   - Decline the certificate for the current cohort and offer the student a reduced rate to join the next cohort with prior work credited
4. The instructor's decision is final. There is no further appeals escalation.

Appeals must be made in good faith. Borderline scores that result from chronic non-engagement (many late submissions, low-effort work throughout) are not appropriate grounds for appeal. Borderline scores that result from one or two difficult weeks in an otherwise strong record are the intended use case.

### Certificate ID Format

Certificate IDs follow this format: **MR-YYYY-XXXXXX**

- **MR** = MISSION REBUILD™ identifier
- **YYYY** = Year of completion (e.g., 2026)
- **XXXXXX** = Sequential 6-digit student number within that year, zero-padded (e.g., 000001, 000042, 000150)

Example: A student who is the 17th graduate of the 2026 cohort receives Certificate ID: **MR-2026-000017**

Maintain a certificate registry spreadsheet with the following columns: Certificate ID | Student Full Name | Completion Date | Cohort | Score | Distinction Level | Issued Date. This record is the authoritative source for certificate verification.

## SECTION 3: CERTIFICATE MEANING AND USE

### What the Certificate Represents

The MISSION REBUILD™ Certificate of Completion is not a participation trophy. Earning it means a student showed up for twelve weeks and did real work — personal, professional, and financial. It represents a completed process of intentional transition: from military identity toward a civilian life built on clarity of purpose, structural discipline, and self-knowledge.

The certificate acknowledges that the recipient:
- Completed a structured 12-week intensive curriculum specifically designed for post-military transition
- Produced documented work in the areas of identity, purpose, structure, financial foundation, career strategy, relationships, community, health, and long-term planning
- Demonstrated the ability to engage in self-directed learning and deliver under a structured accountability system

It does not represent a clinical credential, a license, or a vocational qualification. It represents the completion of a serious course of self-development work — and for many veterans, that is itself a meaningful and hard-won outcome.

## SECTION 4: DISTINCTION LEVELS

### Standard Completion

**Requirement:** All completion criteria met (Section 1). Cumulative score of 75–89/100.

**What it represents:** Full engagement with the course material, all deliverables submitted and passing, participation requirements met. The standard certificate is a meaningful credential and should be treated as such. The existence of distinction levels does not diminish standard completion.

**Certificate notation:** No additional notation. The standard certificate text applies as written.

---

### With Distinction

**Requirement:** All completion criteria met. Cumulative score of 90/100 or above AND instructor's qualitative assessment that the final project represents exceptional engagement, original thinking, or demonstrated breakthrough in a personally challenging area.

The score threshold alone is not sufficient for a Distinction designation. An instructor must make an affirmative judgment that the student's final project goes beyond technical completion. High scores on all assignments with a generic, safe, or surface-level final project do not qualify. Distinction is awarded for work that is specific, honest, and demonstrates real integration of the course material.

**Note on the top-20% framing:** In cohorts of 10 or more students, Distinction should approximate the top 20% of completers by both score and qualitative assessment. In smaller cohorts, this should be interpreted with flexibility — a cohort of four excellent students may produce two or three Distinction graduates, and a cohort with broadly low engagement may produce none.

**Certificate notation:** The words "WITH DISTINCTION" appear on the certificate between the student's name and the course completion statement (as described in Section 2).

**Additional recognition:**
- Personal acknowledgment on the course website's graduate page (with student's permission)
- A brief written commendation from the instructor delivered via email and suitable for use in the student's professional bio or portfolio
- First priority invitation to speak or share their experience in future cohort calls (as a guest graduate)

---

### Mentor Track

**Requirement:** Standard or With Distinction completion, followed by an application and acceptance into the Mentor Track program. Mentor Track is not earned within the 12-week course — it is a post-graduate designation for alumni who choose to contribute to future cohorts.

**Mentor Track candidates:**
- Apply during or within 60 days of completing the course
- Must have a final overall score of 80/100 or above
- Must demonstrate genuine interest in supporting future participants, not in building their own platform through the course

**Mentor Track responsibilities:**
- Serve as a peer resource for one future cohort (available for peer check-ins, discussion contribution, or optional group call appearances)
- Provide written feedback or a recorded reflection on their experience for use in course marketing or orientation materials (with consent)
- Optionally co-facilitate one or more cohort discussion segments, under the lead instructor's guidance

Mentor Track is not a paid or staff position unless separately arranged. It is a voluntary contribution and a meaningful recognition of graduates who want to give back.

**Certificate notation:** The original certificate is not reissued for the Mentor Track designation. Instead, graduates receive a separate Mentor Track letter or supplementary credential, suitable for professional portfolio use, stating:

> "[Student Name] completed the MISSION REBUILD™ course and subsequently served as a Mentor Track contributor to the [Year] Cohort, supporting fellow veterans in their transition through peer engagement and experiential guidance."

**Mentor Track designation listing format:**

For professional documents:

> MISSION REBUILD™ Mentor Track | [Year]

### A Note on Consistency and Fairness

Distinction and Mentor Track designations carry meaning only if they are applied consistently and honestly. Do not award Distinction to a student because they were likeable, because they had a hard story, or because you want to encourage them. Award it because their work earned it. Do not deny it to a student because they were difficult to work with if their work genuinely merited it.

The integrity of these designations protects every graduate — standard and distinguished alike. A certificate that is handed out generously is worth nothing. A certificate that is earned is worth something.
